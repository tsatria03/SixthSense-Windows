unrelease:
You can pause as often as you like, as in the original, instead of only once per stage.
Switching away from the game window pauses a stage, the same as pressing P, as the original did when the phone left the game.
The shop, the weapon list, a weapon's page and the inventory say which screen you are on as they open, instead of only saying "back button".
The inventory no longer equips a weapon you have not bought, so the gold you earn is worth spending. A weapon an old save has switched on can still be switched off.
Rolling from one attack key to the next attacks the lane you just pressed, instead of the one you are still holding.
The key bindings screen asks before it resets every binding. Press R twice to reset, or any other key to keep the bindings you have.
You can rebind a key on the key bindings screen again. Letting go of enter no longer ends the capture before you have pressed the key you want.
You can no longer fire while reloading, and a reload refills the gun you started it with.
Reload no longer works while a zombie holds you, after the game is over, with the grenade or with a blade.
A shot takes half a second to land, as in the original, and a headshot counts if you fired while the zombie's breathing paused.
Shaking the animal zombie off is heard where you are, like a kill of your own, instead of out where the zombie was.
Your breathing, which tells you how hurt you are, comes once every four seconds as in the original, instead of every two.
Zombies move as they walk, so you hear each one come closer and louder in its own lane, and its breathing plays through instead of being cut off at every step.
A zombie that has just appeared is in its own lane straight away, and zombies stop just short of you in their lane instead of in the middle of your head.
Gunshots and knife swings are heard off to the side when you aim at a side lane, the same way a zombie in that lane is heard.
A bullet hitting a zombie is heard where the zombie is, instead of in the middle of your head.
Near the end of each level an alarm sounds and a boss comes straight at you down the middle, and the level only ends once the boss is dead.
Zombies still alive at the end of a level die there instead of following you into the next one, and the ambience changes with the level.
The girl who heals you and the woman zombie appear during a normal game. The girl has her own voice, thanks you when she reaches you, and shooting her costs a heart instead of counting as a kill.
Zombies come as often as in the original, and each section of the corridor starts with a quiet stretch.
You no longer lose health during the tutorial. A zombie that reaches you there starts its lesson again instead.
In the tutorial's shaking step the animal zombie grabs you, so space shakes it off. It can no longer be shot, and the step starts again if it gets you.
The knife and the sword do their normal damage on a headshot, and a hit, a kill and a miss each have their own sound.
When two zombies reach you at once, shaking free always frees you from the one holding you.
Drawing the sword plays its own sound.
Zombies use their full sets of sounds, the woman zombie counts toward the score, and after your first escape in a stage later grabs break with one shake, as in the original.
The game lets go of each music and ambience track it has finished with, so a long session no longer grows in memory as the levels change.
The pause panel's main menu row says "main menu button" in full, from the original recording, instead of a cut that stopped after "main menu".
Gunshots are as loud as in the original, and killing a zombie plays its kill sound.
Dying plays the death sound where you fell, instead of saying "game over" twice.
A key you were holding when you left a stage no longer stays pressed in the next one, and nothing stays stuck after you switch away from the window.
The game no longer opens a second audio device beside the one it plays through.
The menu music carries on where it was when you come back to the menu, instead of starting over from the beginning.
The main menu's music is quieter, so the menu's own voice can be heard over it.
"Now Loading" now always plays before the menu music stops, and the level's own sound and the tutorial's first prompt wait for it to finish.
Leaving the tutorial now stops whatever prompt was still playing, instead of letting it run into the menu.
Replaying the tutorial from the menu no longer reads it as already finished.
Pressing P during the tutorial now stops its prompts and its timer, instead of leaving them running.
The tutorial's prompts no longer restart every second once they have already played through once.
The "no coin" message no longer speaks its own sentence over the recording that already says it.
The "you must use earphones" warning now plays once, from the opening screen, instead of colliding with the main menu's title or repeating every time you start a game.
Moving to another row in the shop, the inventory or the main menu now stops whatever that row was saying, instead of letting it run into the next one.
The Try button on a weapon's page now says the weapon test range is not available, instead of doing nothing and saying nothing.
The game now comes with the licenses for OpenAL Soft, the NVDA controller client, Prism and pygame, in a licenses folder beside the game.
The key bindings screen and the main menu's spoken messages now work with JAWS, ZoomText, System Access, Narrator and other screen readers as well as NVDA, and with a Windows voice when no screen reader is running.
The game's sounds are sorted into folders inside game\sounds, each under its original name.
Switching weapons keeps each weapon's own ammo, instead of refilling the magazine for free.
Leaving a stage stops the zombie footsteps, the ambience and the music, instead of letting them play on under the menu.
The rain in the third area keeps falling for the whole level.
The level music and ambience play as quietly as in the original game, so you can hear the zombies over them.
Numbers are spoken in the right order, so ten says "one, zero" instead of "zero, one".
Starting a game before you have finished the tutorial now takes you to the tutorial without spending a coin, instead of leaving you standing still in silence.
The coin row on the main menu can say how long until your next coin, instead of always reading zero, and it no longer reads your coins after you have already moved to another row.
Spending a coin no longer restarts a countdown that is already running.
A coin now comes back every 30 minutes instead of every 10, up to 5, and the coins you missed while the game was closed are given back when you return.
A new player now starts with 10 coins.
